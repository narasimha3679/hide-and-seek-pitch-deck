Logline

Harsha spent his life making people believe in things that weren’t real. But after losing his daughter, a brilliant illusion designer becomes trapped in an illusion of his own.

Chapter 01 · The man on the stage

The Impossible Crimes

Fortunes vanish from heavily guarded locations, yet the evidence never explains how. Witnesses insist they were watching. Security followed procedure. Somehow the crucial moment happened right before their eyes.

One officer, George, begins to suspect the mastermind isn’t just planning robberies. He is designing what people believe they see. His trail leads to a sold-out theatre, and a celebrated magician named Harsha.

A thief looks for a weakness in a lock. A magician looks for a weakness in human attention.

Chapter 02 · Ninety-one… ninety-two…

The Next Operation

“That’s cheating!”

After the show, Harsha briefs his gang. The officer is right: the celebrated magician is the architect of the robberies. Every man has a role, every cue a time. One worries the officer is getting close. Harsha refuses to cancel.

Then, one night in his sprawling mansion, a little girl steals a chocolate. Caught with the wrapper in her hand, she laughs and dares him to hide-and-seek. He covers his eyes and counts. “Twenty-one… twenty-two…” Then, mischievously, he skips ahead.

While he keeps counting, she races through the rooms looking for the perfect hiding place. At last she notices the enormous refrigerator, its shelves filled with chocolates.

She remembers a magic trick: someone steps into a cabinet and reappears elsewhere. Perhaps she can disappear just like that.

A perfect hiding place?

Headlights across the gates

Police vehicles race through the night, radios crackling. They are not heading to another robbery. The officer and his team rush into Harsha’s mansion to arrest the magician.

Harsha insists a little girl is hiding somewhere in the house. He was only playing a game. He has to find her. The police search the rooms as he is taken into custody. Nobody answers their calls. His confidence gives way to fear.

At last, an officer enters the kitchen and opens the refrigerator. What began as a playful game ends in an unbearable tragedy.

She went in expecting magic. He never knew she was there.

Chapter 04 · The edge of his world

The Girl Who Returns

The threshold

Eight months later, the magician who once held thousands spellbound has abandoned the stage. Harsha lives alone in the vast mansion, among unused equipment and unfinished designs. One night he hears a child’s laughter, follows it down a corridor, and sees the same little girl running away, daring him to find her.

He laughs, chases her, and plays hide-and-seek as though that terrible night never happened. But no one else can see her. She asks: “Will you take me to see a magic show?” One day he gets ready, opens the front door and steps outside. She doesn’t follow. He walks back; she smiles as though nothing is wrong. The moment he crosses the threshold, she remains behind.

The girl can exist for him only inside this mansion. If he wants her to experience magic, he cannot take her to a theatre. He will have to bring the theatre to her.

The girl can exist only in his imagination

The Girl Who Shouldn’t Be Alive

Now we enter the home of the officer who arrested Harsha. His investigation consumes his days. Then a little girl walks into the room. It is the very same girl we saw dead in Harsha’s refrigerator. She speaks. He answers. She moves through the house as if nothing terrible has happened.

How is she alive? Was the death not what it seemed?

Chapter 06 · Tonight, the show begins

A Theatre Inside a Home

Back in the present, Harsha designs again, but not for a public auditorium. He is transforming his own mansion into one. Walls hide passages, rooms change appearance, light and sound lead visitors to believe they are somewhere else entirely.

The girl watches with excitement. “Tonight, I’m going to show you my magic.” She asks the obvious question: “But where are the people?” Outside, vehicles begin to arrive.

Chapter 07 · One night. Three stories.

The Chase We Didn’t See

To understand the guest list, we return to the night everything went wrong. Pick a story.

The Theatre

One evening, George brings his little daughter to one of Harsha’s shows. She watches in wonder as a person enters a cabinet, vanishes, and reappears somewhere else. It is an image she will never forget.

The Road

After the show, George follows Harsha’s car. It stops on a lonely stretch of road. Through the windows, the seats look empty; inside, Harsha sits calmly smoking, concealed by an illusion in the glass. Unable to see him, George returns to his own car and looks into the back seat. His daughter is gone. Only her doll remains. When he turns back, Harsha’s car has vanished too.

The Mansion

Harsha took the girl from George’s car on purpose, drawing him away from a major police operation scheduled that night. While George mobilised every resource to search for his missing child, the gang could rob without interference. Harsha even left the clue that brought the police to his mansion. He expected them to come. What he never intended was the little girl’s death.

Characters

Two minds. One truth.

The officer is Harsha’s intellectual equal and emotional opposite. Turn each card.

The master of illusion

HARSHA

Age 30 · Magician
Brilliant · Manipulative · Unpredictable

Harsha

A celebrated magician who understands one dangerous truth: people don’t always believe what they see; they see what they are made to believe. Behind the stage lies a calculating criminal mind that turns misdirection, timing and psychological manipulation into weapons.

When his plan leads to the death of an innocent little girl, the man who mastered illusions becomes a prisoner of his own mind. He is neither hero nor straightforward villain: capable of creating the impossible, but powerless to undo the one tragedy he caused.

The relentless investigator

GEORGE

Age 35–40 · Police officer
Determined · Observant · Fearless

George, the officer

A brilliant investigator who recognises patterns others overlook. When impossible robberies baffle the police, he is convinced someone is deliberately manipulating what people believe they see. With no concrete evidence, he refuses to abandon his suspicion of Harsha.

Beneath the discipline is a devoted father. When his daughter disappears, professional determination becomes personal obsession. Harsha manipulates reality to conceal the truth; George is determined to expose it, even when it threatens to destroy him.

Tone & crossover

The Prestige meets Don’t Breathe

Period-theatre elegance and the mechanics of misdirection, locked inside the pressure of a single house where no one is safe.

Candlelit theatre

Gold light against deep black. Spotlights, curtains and chandeliers make every room feel like a stage, and every stage feel like a trap.

Practical illusion

Hidden passages, rooms that change, glass that hides a car. The audience is shown how misdirection works, then fooled anyway.

03

One house

The mansion is the stage, the maze and the witness. Pressure builds as every person with a piece of the truth shares one roof.
